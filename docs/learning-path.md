# The 10-hour reading path

A short course for curiosity, not completion. Four two-hour chapters form the core. The last two hours are reserved for making connections, questioning what you have read, and deciding what deserves more attention.

<div class="chapter-info"><span>10 hours total</span><span>Four chapters</span><span>No prerequisites beyond curiosity</span></div>

## The synthesis studio · 2 hours

### Hour one — four ideas, four paragraphs

Write a paragraph on each of the following. Explain the idea without copying the vocabulary of the source.

- **Reasoning:** What distinguishes a plausible explanation from a logically necessary conclusion?
- **Formal limits:** Why does Gödel's work not justify the claim that all reasoning is fundamentally unreliable?
- **Relativity:** What can observers disagree about, and what remains invariant?
- **Entropy:** Why does an irreversible macroscopic trend not require every microscopic process to be time-asymmetric?

### Hour two — connect the chapters

Choose **two** of the questions below and write a half-page response to each.

1. Is a scientific law a logical truth, an empirical regularity, or something else?
2. How do mathematical models and physical observations constrain each other?
3. What is the difference between a limitation of a formal proof system and uncertainty about a physical theory?
4. Why is the explanation of the arrow of time not the same problem as the relativity of simultaneity?

<div class="chapter-callout"><strong>Output, not performance</strong>It is enough to finish with a page of questions worth keeping. A short, well-argued note is better than a comprehensive summary.</div>

## How to keep notes

Save notes beside each chapter, or in a separate personal notebook. Use a consistent structure: **claim · argument · counterexample · remaining question**. Keep unpublished thoughts separate if you prefer; the book itself can remain a public reading guide.
