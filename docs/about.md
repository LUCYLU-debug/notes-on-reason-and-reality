# About the book

*Notes on Reason & Reality* is a small, evolving collection of study guides. Its organising idea is simple: before asking what the world is like, it helps to ask how conclusions are reached. And once the tools of reasoning are in view, it becomes possible to ask what they can establish—and what the physical sciences reveal that ordinary intuition misses.

The chapters are deliberately short. Each begins with a question, introduces a few carefully chosen resources, and ends with work to do independently. The point is not to consume a long reading list. It is to think with the material.

## Why this order?

**Reason and knowledge** comes first because a claim about the world needs an account of the grounds for believing it.

**Limits of formal thought** follows because even rigorous systems have assumptions, expressive limits, and carefully stated theorems about what can be derived.

**Physical reality** comes next because the world can correct our intuitions: relativity makes time relational, while statistical mechanics provides an account of the direction we observe in macroscopic processes.

This is one useful route through the material, not the only intellectually defensible one.

## What might come next?

- **Scientific explanation** — causation, models, realism, and scientific change
- **Mind and experience** — perception, consciousness, and personal identity
- **Mathematics and structure** — infinity, probability, and symmetry
- **Ethics and human life** — what can and cannot follow from facts alone
